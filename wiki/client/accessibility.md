# Accessibility

Two windows: **Accessibility** (Menu > Accessibility, or Settings > Accessibility...) for size, colours and motion, and **Controls** (`H`, the top bar's *Controls* button, Settings > Controls..., Menu > Controls) for keys. Every setting is saved the moment it changes, per device, in the platform's preferences (`morgana-accessibility`: a file on desktop, local storage in a browser) by `AccessibilityStore`, and read back at start by `AccessibilityController.load`. The current settings are in the `Accessibility` object, which the HUD, the renderer and the input code read.

## Accessibility window
- **Text and interface size** (`UiScales`): 75%, 100%, 125%, 150%, 175% or 200%. It scales the whole Scene2D interface (`ScreenViewport.unitsPerPixel`), windows are kept on screen, and it is now remembered (it used to reset every start). The Settings window's "Zoom" button is the same setting.
- **Colour set** (`ColourPalette`): Standard, **Red-green safe** (Okabe and Ito colours), **Blue-yellow safe** (Tol's bright colours) and **High contrast**. The game asks for a role (`PaletteRole`: health, second, third and fourth bar, good, bad, hostile, friendly, other player, explored) and the palette says what it looks like. Roles now follow the palette on the vital bars (top bar and compact status), the overhead health bars, damage and healing numbers (`PaletteColours.floatingText` turns the server's clearly red or green number colours into the palette's own), the minimap blips (hostile ones are also larger than friendly ones, so size is a second cue) and the world map. Swatches in the window preview a set before choosing it, and bars always show their numbers, so colour is never the only information.
- **Auto-loot** (`AccessibilitySettings.autoLoot`, off by default; `Change.AUTO_LOOT`): `MorganaGame.tickAutoLoot` asks `AutoLootModel` every frame whether to send the ordinary `PickupGroundItem` request. It sends when loot within `GROUND_PICKUP_RANGE_TILES` is unowned or the player's own and fits the bag (`Inventory.addStack`); loot another player still owns is left for the pickup key. The same loot is not requested again for 3 s unless the loot around changes, so a refusal cannot become a request stream. The server is unchanged: it still checks range, ownership and room. Test: `AutoLootModelTest`.
- **Reduce motion** (`Accessibility.reduceMotion`): vital bars jump instead of easing and a low bar is dimmer instead of pulsing, no draining trail, cooldown buttons do not flash, windows and toasts appear and go without fading, the quest waypoint stops bobbing, the minimap and world map blips stay solid, damage numbers do not rise or pop, and falling rain and snow become one faint still tint. It starts on when the operating system or browser asks for less motion (`prefers-reduced-motion`, read by `WebLauncher` and passed as `MorganaGame.systemReducedMotion`) and stays wherever the player puts it afterwards.

## Controls window (keys)
Every action has two key slots (`GameAction`, grouped by `ActionGroup`: four movement directions; attack, interact, pick up; inventory, spellbook, quest log, party, minimap, world map, the Controls window itself (`H`); and quickbar slots 1 to 10), starting as the keys the game always used (WASD and arrows, Space and Left Ctrl, E and Enter, the number row and numpad for the quickbar, ...). It shows two columns (movement, actions and windows; quickbar and fixed keys) when the screen is wide enough and one scrolling column otherwise; each quickbar row also shows what the slot holds and the window says how to fill a slot (+Bar in the Spellbook, Quickbar in the Inventory). Click a key button, press the new key; **Esc** cancels, **Backspace** removes that slot's key; *Reset all keys* restores the standard layout. Only `F1` and `Esc` are fixed. The quickbar buttons show each slot's key in short form (`KeyBindings.shortKeyName`: `1`, `Q`, `Np1`, `LCtr`). A layout saved before the quickbar keys were rebindable loads with the quickbar on its default keys. The window is rebuilt only when keys or quickbar contents change (rebuilding it every frame replaced buttons in the middle of a click). `KeyBindings` (immutable, pure, `KeyBindingsTest`) enforces: a key does one thing (taking a key from another action says so, unless it was that action's only key, then it is refused), every action keeps at least one key, and a saved layout that is damaged, hand-edited or in conflict is repaired on load, so movement can never be lost. Only changed actions are saved. The key hints on the top buttons and the minimap title follow the bindings. A movement key that types nothing (arrows, modifiers) still works while a text box has focus, a letter does not (`isNonTextKey`).

## Where it is checked
- `AccessibilityTest.kt`: key rules, save and load, damage tolerance, the controller's press-a-key flow, settings round trip.
- **The palettes are tested against simulated colour blindness** (`PaletteColoursTest`): the pure test applies the Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes matrices for protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia and measures CIELAB distance. It proves the standard palette really is confusable (health against the third bar, damage against healing under deuteranopia), and requires every palette's bars, good against bad, and minimap blips to stay at least 25 (red-green set, for protan and deutan) or 20 (blue-yellow set, for all three) apart; the high contrast set must reach a 7:1 contrast ratio against a dark background. When a colour was changed the test found the problem first (a purple fourth bar looked blue to a protanope; tritanopes confuse blue with green), and the bar colours of the blue-yellow set were picked by searching a pool of safe colours for the best separation.
- The browser smoke test opens the window, rebinds Pick up from G to B, resets keys, cycles the colour sets and closes it ([browser smoke test](/playbooks/browser_smoke_test.md)).

## Not covered yet
Screen-reader-friendly chat (an ARIA live region in the browser), gamepad support, colour-blind palettes and reduce motion in the F1 editors (they have their own colour schemes), sprite and tile animation and water shimmer (art, not UI), sound captions, a font-size setting separate from the whole-interface size, hold-to-toggle and one-handed layouts, and a "prefers-reduced-motion" for Android.
